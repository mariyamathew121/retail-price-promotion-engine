Retail Price & Promotion Decision Engine

Proof and What-if

Two views of the same work: how accurate the sales forecasts are, and what a price change would predict for one product.

Read this first. The forecast check measures prediction accuracy on past data. The what-if view shows a scenario based on estimated price response. Neither shows that a price change will cause the predicted result. Use the what-if view to choose a product for a controlled test, not to set a price.

1. How accurate are the sales forecasts?

Each test hides the most recent weeks of sales, predicts them, and compares the forecast with what actually happened. Lower error is better. The simple method repeats the average of the last four weeks.

The 13-week test was used while the model was being developed, so the 26- and 52-week tests are the cleaner check. Error is the total absolute miss divided by total units sold.

2. What would a price change do?

Choose a product and move the price. The estimate uses how demand responded to price for that product in the past, with the range of plausible responses shown.
